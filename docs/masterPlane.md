Master Plan & Development Roadmap

A flexible personal system for turning long-term direction into meaningful daily action.

Build a life intentionally, not just complete daily tasks. Connect your long-term direction to learning intentions, milestones, habits, daily actions, and regular reflection.

Prepared as the shared reference for the personal growth system and the Angular application that will support it.

1. Purpose, principles, and life vision

Purpose: create a practical system that helps you decide what matters now, make steady progress, notice what you are learning, and adjust your direction without losing sight of your bigger ambitions.

Operating principles

Direction over pressure: the plan gives you direction; it should not become another source of stress.

Learning before performance when a skill is new: focus on capability first, then measure outcomes when the skill becomes more established.

Small, repeatable actions: build routines that can survive busy weeks.

Flexible priorities: not every important life area needs daily attention.

Evidence over vague progress: record what you completed, understood, built, or practiced.

No shame-based streaks: missing a day does not erase previous progress.

Review and adapt: goals can be refined, paused, replaced, or moved to the future.

2. Planning hierarchy and priority statuses

Level	Planning question	Typical horizon

Life vision	What kind of life and capabilities am I moving toward?	3–5 years and beyond

Current phase	What deserves focused attention in this season?	8–12 weeks

Goals & intentions	What outcome or capability am I developing?	Weeks to months

Milestones	What observable progress will show that I am moving forward?	Weekly or monthly

Daily actions	What is the next useful step I can take today?	Today

Reflection	What did I learn, and what should change?	Daily / weekly / monthly

Priority statuses

Status	Meaning	Example

ACTIVE FOCUS	One to three areas receiving most of your deliberate effort in the current phase.	Career transition and full-stack development.

MAINTENANCE	Small actions that keep an important area alive without demanding major effort.	English practice, faith routine, light reading.

FUTURE	Important directions intentionally parked until the right time.	AI/ML specialization, a master’s application, starting another language.

PRIORITY RULE

Keep the number of Active Focus areas small. Move a goal between statuses during a weekly or monthly review. A goal in Future is not abandoned; it is simply not competing for attention right now.

3. Initial 12-week personal roadmap

The first 12 weeks establish a direction and a review cycle. The plan assumes that work or a changing schedule may affect available time, so weekly progress matters more than rigid daily quotas.

Phase 1 — Establish the foundation (Weeks 1–4)

Set a realistic baseline routine and identify the parts of the day that are actually available.

Clarify the current work and financial priorities; adapt the weekly load if a job begins.

Study the end-to-end Angular/.NET project playlist to understand the whole application flow before deep-diving into individual topics.

Refresh programming fundamentals, OOP, SQL concepts, and the relationship between frontend, backend, APIs, and databases.

Continue the McKinsey Forward learning journey and keep short notes on useful ideas.

Maintain practical English speaking/listening practice and a small personal reflection habit.

Phase 1 milestone: a sustainable weekly rhythm, a clear picture of the full-stack application architecture, and a prioritized list of technical gaps.

Phase 2 — Build capability (Weeks 5–8)

Deepen Angular and TypeScript: components, routing, forms, state, services, and API integration.

Strengthen C# and [ASP.NET](http://ASP.NET) Core fundamentals: OOP, dependency injection, controllers or minimal APIs, validation, and service structure.

Practice database design and SQL queries; understand how data moves through the application.

Refresh data structures and algorithms with consistent, manageable practice.

Rebuild small parts of the tutorial project or create focused exercises without copying every step.

Continue English and personal development as Maintenance activities unless they become a current priority.

Phase 2 milestone: independently implement and explain small frontend, backend, and database features.

Phase 3 — Apply, demonstrate, and evaluate (Weeks 9–12)

Build or extend a complete application feature with your own decisions and implementation.

Connect frontend, backend, and database; validate important user flows.

Learn deployment fundamentals and get a working version online where feasible.

Explore Git workflow, Docker, CI/CD, and cloud concepts in a sequence that supports the project rather than distracting from it.

Review career readiness, portfolio gaps, and the next set of technical learning intentions.

Decide whether AI/ML should remain a Future direction or become part of the next planning phase.

Phase 3 milestone: a demonstrable application or substantial feature, clearer technical confidence, and a reviewed next-quarter plan.

Milestone evidence

Checkpoint	Evidence to save

Week 4	Architecture notes, list of skill gaps, first routine review, learning log entries.

Week 8	Small features or exercises implemented independently; notes explaining key concepts.

Week 12	Working project/demo, deployment notes if completed, updated portfolio and next-phase backlog.

4. Goal-setting and learning framework

Learning intention vs. performance goal

A learning intention describes a capability or understanding you want to develop. A performance goal describes an outcome you want to achieve. Use learning intentions when a skill is new; use performance goals more heavily once the skill is established.

Area	Learning intention	Performance goal

Full-stack development	Understand how Angular, [ASP.NET](http://ASP.NET) Core, APIs, and a database work together.	Build and deploy a full-stack application.

English	Improve the ability to organize thoughts and speak clearly in real time.	Complete an interview or work conversation with confidence.

Adaptability	Recognize limiting mindsets and practice reframing.	Apply APR to a real challenge and choose a constructive next action.

Recommended goal structure

1. Domain: which life area does this belong to?

2. Why it matters: what personal meaning does this goal have?

3. Learning intention: what do I want to understand or become able to do?

4. Performance outcome: what result would demonstrate progress, if applicable?

5. Milestones: what smaller evidence points will show progress?

6. Supporting habits: what repeatable actions make progress easier?

7. Review date: when will I reconsider the goal and its priority?

Habit loop

Element	Question	Example

Cue	What reminder or situation starts the habit?	After breakfast, open the learning note.

Routine	What is the small action?	Study one concept or solve one focused exercise.

Reward	What makes the habit feel complete or satisfying?	Tick it off and write one thing learned.

DESIGN RULE

Make the smallest useful version of a habit easy to complete. Use a full session when time allows, but keep a minimum version for busy days. The app should track consistency without turning a missed day into failure.

5. Mindset practice: seven shifts and APR

The mindset module turns the McKinsey framework into a repeatable self-reflection tool. It is for learning and personal reflection, not for diagnosing personality or mental health.

Limiting mindset	More adaptable alternative	Practice prompt

Fixed	Growth	What could I learn from this attempt or mistake?

Expert	Curious	What question could I ask, or what can I try to understand?

Reactive	Creative	What possibilities or experiments have I not considered?

Victim	Agent	What part of this situation can I influence or act on?

Scarcity	Abundance	Could there be a win-win option or another resource?

Certainty	Exploration	How can I plan while staying open to new information?

Protection	Opportunity	What useful opportunity could exist alongside the risk?

APR: Awareness → Pause → Reframe

1. Awareness: notice the mindset or first interpretation you are using.

2. Pause: create a moment before automatically reacting.

3. Reframe: consider a more adaptable perspective and choose one reasonable next action.

Reflection entry

Situation: what happened?

Initial interpretation: what did I immediately think?

Mindset noticed: which of the seven patterns might be present?

Alternative perspective: what else could be true?

Next action: what small step can I take?

6. Daily, weekly, and monthly operating rhythm

Daily planning

Daily element	What to record

Daily intention	One sentence describing how you want to approach the day or what matters most.

Top priorities	Up to three meaningful tasks; keep the list short.

Habits & routines	A few repeatable actions linked to current goals.

Learning block	Topic, time spent, and one thing understood or practiced.

End-of-day reflection	What moved forward? What did I learn? What needs to carry over?

Weekly review

What did I complete or move forward?

What did I learn or understand better?

What felt difficult, distracting, or unrealistic?

Which priorities deserve more, less, or different attention?

What are the three most useful outcomes for next week?

Monthly / phase review

Review active goals and their connection to the life vision.

Check milestone evidence rather than relying only on how productive the month felt.

Move goals between Active Focus, Maintenance, and Future.

Retire or rewrite goals that no longer make sense.

Choose the next phase and update the backlog.

Time budget for the app-building project

Session type	Duration	Use

Minimum	20 minutes	Review the last step, fix one small issue, or record what you learned.

Normal	About 1 hour	Concept review, a small coding task, independent modification, and a quick test.

Extended	Up to 2 hours	A larger feature broken into small steps, with time for testing and notes.

Suggested cadence: around five sessions per week when practical. This is a guideline, not an attendance requirement. When work or life becomes busy, reduce the session size and resume from the last clear step instead of trying to catch up.

Suggested one-hour work session

Part	Time	Purpose

Review	10 min	Recall where the project stopped.

Understand	15 min	Learn the concept needed for the next task.

Build	25 min	Implement a small, bounded piece with assistance.

Own it	5 min	Change or explain something independently.

Log	5 min	Test and record what was learned or left unfinished.

7. Personal Growth OS application: product scope

Product concept: a personal life and learning management system, not just a to-do list or streak-based habit tracker. It connects long-term goals with current priorities and the daily actions that support them.

Primary user experience

Section	Core responsibility	MVP capability

Dashboard	Show the current picture at a glance.	Active goals, daily intention, today’s priorities, habit snapshot.

Goals	Organize life domains, long-term directions, current phases, and milestones.	Create/edit goals, assign domain and status, add milestones.

Daily Planner	Turn priorities into manageable actions.	Daily tasks, habits, completion state, brief reflection.

Mindset	Support awareness and APR reflection.	Seven mindset pairs, guided APR entry, reflection history.

Learning	Track learning intentions and learning activity.	Intentions, resources/notes, learning log, progress evidence.

Progress	Make progress visible and support reviews.	Weekly summaries, milestone status, simple trend views.

Core relationships

A Life Domain can contain multiple Goals.

A Goal can have a current phase, one or more Learning Intentions, Milestones, and supporting Habits.

A Daily Plan contains tasks and habit records linked to goals when relevant.

A Learning Log records what was studied, practiced, built, or understood.

A Reflection can be linked to a day, a goal, or an APR entry.

MVP boundaries

BUILD FIRST

Goals & Learning Intentions; Habits & Daily Planner; Mindset & APR; Basic Dashboard & Progress; local persistent storage; data export/import.

DEFER UNTIL THE CORE WORKS

AI reflection companion, cloud accounts, cross-device sync, social features, advanced analytics, complex notifications, and backend services. These belong in a future backlog, not the first release.

Backend (future)	[ASP.NET](http://ASP.NET) Core Web API	Introduce when sync, accounts, or remote services are genuinely needed.

Cloud / delivery (future)	Free-tier hosting initially; Azure, Docker, CI/CD later	Learn deployment incrementally without making the MVP depend on paid services.

9. Application development roadmap: sprints and milestones

The application is built in small Agile increments. Each sprint should end with something usable, tested, and understood. Sprint length can vary; do not force a fixed calendar if learning takes longer.

Sprint	Scope	Deliverable / acceptance evidence

Sprint 0 — Foundation	Create Angular app, establish Git repository, review Standalone structure, configure routing and basic responsive shell.	App runs locally; first commit exists; folder structure and architecture notes are in place.

Sprint 1 — Dashboard shell	Create navigation and dashboard layout with placeholder cards and empty states.	Responsive navigation works; dashboard shows the current focus and planned sections.

Sprint 2 — Goals & intentions	Life domains, goals, status, learning intentions, milestones, create/edit forms.	A goal can be created, edited, categorized, prioritized, and connected to milestones.

Sprint 3 — Habits & planner	Daily entries, tasks, habit tracking, daily intention, local persistence.	Daily data remains after refresh; user can complete and review daily actions.

Sprint 4 — Mindset & APR	Seven mindset pairs, guided APR form, reflection history.	An APR reflection can be created, saved, and reviewed.

Sprint 5 — Progress & review	Weekly review, learning log, milestone summaries, simple charts if useful.	User can review the week and see evidence of progress.

Sprint 6 — PWA & resilience	Responsive refinement, offline checks, export/import, usability and accessibility testing.	Installability/offline behavior is tested on target devices; backup and restore are verified.

Sprint workflow

1. Planning: choose one useful outcome and split it into small tasks.

2. Development: implement the smallest working slice.

3. Testing: verify the main flow, empty states, edge cases, and saved data.

4. Review: demonstrate what works and record what remains.

5. Retrospective: identify one thing to keep and one thing to improve in the next sprint.

Definition of Done

The feature works for its intended main flow.

The layout is usable on desktop and mobile sizes.

Data persistence behaves as expected where applicable.

Important edge cases and empty states have been checked.

The code is organized in the appropriate feature area.

The developer can explain the important code and make a small change independently.

The learning log and sprint log are updated.

10. Agile workflow, quality rules, and project documents

How to use AI while learning

Ask for the concept and design first; do not jump straight to a large code dump.

Agree on the smallest useful implementation before writing code.

Generate or review code in small pieces that can be tested.

Ask for explanations of decisions, data flow, and trade-offs.

Modify at least one part independently after the assisted implementation.

Do not accept a feature as finished if its important parts cannot be explained.

12. Review and adaptation rules

When life changes

If work begins or the schedule changes, reduce the number of active goals before reducing sleep or essential recovery time.

If a week is missed, resume from the last clear step. Do not create a catch-up backlog of every missed task.

If a goal feels too large, rewrite it as a learning intention and define a smaller next action.

If a goal no longer matters, pause or archive it with a short note instead of forcing it to remain active.

If an area is important but cannot be a focus now, move it to Maintenance or Future.

If the app itself becomes burdensome, simplify the system before adding more features.

Success after the first 12 weeks

Success does not mean every item is complete. A successful first cycle means you have a workable routine, know what your current priorities are, can identify the next useful steps, have recorded evidence of learning, and have a usable first version of the system or a clear path to it.

THE DECISION RULE

Choose the next action that best supports the current phase. Protect flexibility. Keep the system useful enough that you want to return to it.

Immediate next steps — Sprint 0

1. Create the Angular project using Standalone architecture.

2. Run the project locally and understand the generated structure.

3. Create the Git repository and make the first commit.

4. Set up the initial feature-based folders and app routes.

5. Create a simple responsive application shell with navigation placeholders.

6. Write the first README and record the architecture decisions.

The first development session should focus on understanding and setup, not building the entire application at once.
